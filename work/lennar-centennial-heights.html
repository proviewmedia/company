Lennar, Centennial Heights

Townhomes buyers could walk around before they ever drove out.

A film, a 3D capture of the exterior, a 360 tour of the interior, and an aerial still set. One visit to Centennial Hills.

The film

Cut for a phone, not a lobby screen.

Walkthrough film, Centennial Heights The community and the homes in one continuous read, cut to a length that holds on a phone and works as a paid placement without a re-edit.

The 3D capture

Drag it. The whole building is there.

Aerial passes flown around the block and reconstructed into a 3D scene you can orbit in a browser. No app, no plugin, no download.

Interactive 3D scene, drag to orbit Elevation, roofline, setbacks, garage placement, and how the units sit against each other. Questions a floor plan cannot answer and a single photograph will not settle.

The interior tour

Inside, room by room, on the buyer's own time.

A 360 walkthrough of the interior. Buyers move between rooms themselves, look up, look back, and stand where they want to stand, instead of watching someone else's camera path.

360 interior walkthrough, drag to look around The rooms a floor plan lists but does not let anyone stand in. Out of state buyers get the interior without booking a flight, and the tour stays up long after a model home closes for the evening.

The situation

Townhomes are hard to sell on a listing page. Photographs flatten them. A buyer cannot tell how the unit sits in the block, how tall it reads from the street, or what the approach actually looks like, and the model home only answers part of it.

Out of state buyers have the same problem with the drive. Centennial Hills is a thirty minute reality that a map pin does not communicate. Both gaps get solved by showing the place instead of describing it.

What we did

Four disciplines, one visit.

Video production

Shot list built around the buyer's sequence. Filmed, graded, and cut to a length that survives a phone screen and a short attention span.

Aerial capture

Flown under FAA Part 107, airspace cleared against the address before the date was set. Orbits and elevation passes covering the block from every side.

3D reconstruction

The aerial passes reconstructed into a navigable 3D scene that loads in a browser and runs on a phone. Embeddable anywhere a link goes.

360 interior

The interior shot as connected panoramas, so a buyer walks the rooms in their own order rather than following a fixed camera path.

The aerials

Stills out of the same flights.

The passes that built the 3D scene also produce a still set, so the listing, the brochure, and the ad have images that match the film.

Why it matters

One visit, four deliverables.

The same day produces all of it

Film, 3D scene, 360 interior, and stills come out of one visit, rather than a video crew on one date, a photographer on another, and a tour vendor on a third.

Outside and inside both answered

The 3D capture settles elevation, orientation, and how the units sit together. The 360 tour settles what the rooms feel like. Neither question waits on a site visit.

It travels

The film runs on the listing, on social, and in paid placements. The 3D scene and the tour embed on a community page or drop into an email as links that open on any device.

Outcome

Lennar left with a film, an interactive 3D capture of the exterior, a 360 tour of the interior, and an aerial still set of the Centennial Heights townhomes. All from one production, and all usable on a listing, in an email, or in an ad without further work.
