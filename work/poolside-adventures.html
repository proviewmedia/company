Poolside Adventures, Bloomington Country Club

A product too big to photograph from the deck.

Four aerial films of an in-pool climbing structure, shot where it is installed rather than against a white background.

Aerial partner on a production by Elite Media Productions, Las Vegas. Flown under FAA Part 107.

The situation

Poolside Adventures sells structures that go in the water. The buyer is a club, a resort, or an HOA board, and the question they are actually asking is not what the product looks like. It is how much of the pool it takes, how it sits next to the lanes and the lifeguard stand, and whether it looks like an amenity or like clutter.

A catalog photograph on white cannot answer any of that. Neither can a shot from the deck, because from the deck you are standing too close and too low to see the thing in relation to anything else. The product only reads from the air.

The product

The structure, at eye level with itself.

Flown level with the top of the climb, which is where the scale finally lands. The pool reads as a pool, and the structure reads as something a person could climb.

Pool overview Red rock behind, the club behind that. A buyer sees the product and the venue in the same frame, which is the argument.

The orbits

All the way around, twice.

Two full orbits. One tight on the structure and the deck it occupies, one wide enough to put the whole aquatics area in context. Between them there is no angle a board member can ask about that is not already answered.

Accessory orbit The footprint. How much deck it takes, how it sits against the lanes, where the chairs still fit.

Pool area orbit The same move pulled back. Aquatics area, clubhouse, course, and the valley the club sits in.

The venue

The club the product is selling into.

Bloomington Country Club carries the pitch as much as the structure does. A prospect running a property like this one sees their own club in the footage.

Club aerial Clubhouse, aquatics, and the course, so the footage doubles as property material for the club itself.

Our part

The aerial, on someone else's production.

Elite Media Productions had the client and the brief. What the job needed was a certified pilot who could turn the brief into flyable moves on the day.

Part 107 pilot in command

Airspace checked against the address before the date was confirmed, and the flight flown under FAA Part 107. The certificate is the reason the call came, and carrying it is the pilot's responsibility, not the production's.

Shot list into flight plan

A product overview at the height of the climb, two orbits at different radii, and a club aerial. Shot early, while the deck was clear and the water was still.

Four cuts, not one

Delivered as separate films rather than a single edit, so a sales deck, a product page, and a social post each get the one that fits instead of a trim of the same clip.

Why it matters

The venue is part of the product.

Installed beats isolated

A structure photographed on white is a shape. The same structure in a working club pool is a decision someone else already made, which is a far easier thing to sell against.

Scale stops being a guess

Deck space, sight lines, and how the pool still functions around it. These are the objections that stall a purchase, and aerial answers them without a phone call.

Studios can hire the certificate

Elite Media Productions did not need a second production company. They needed a Part 107 pilot who would show up, fly the brief, and hand back usable footage. That is a call we are set up to take.

Outcome

Four aerial films of the structure in place at Bloomington Country Club: a product overview, two orbits, and a club aerial. One morning of flying, delivered into Elite Media Productions' engagement with Poolside Adventures, and usable on a product page, in a sales deck, or as a paid placement without a re-edit.
