Industries

Golf courses, resorts, and venues whose grounds are the product.

When the property is the product, a photograph is a poor way to sell it. A course is a few hundred acres, a resort is a dozen buildings and everything between them, and a venue is a room somebody has to picture their wedding inside. One frame picks one angle and asks the visitor to take the rest on faith.

Most of the deciding happens before anyone drives out. A planner comparing four venues, a golfer choosing a course to travel for, a couple booking from two states away. They are all looking at a website at ten at night, and whichever property shows them the most is the one that gets the call.

Questions

What courses and venues ask.

Can you shoot without closing the course?

Yes. We fly early, before play or before an event load in, which is also when the light is worth having. Nothing needs to shut down.

Do you shoot every hole?

We can. Hole by hole is a common scope for a course, and it doubles as the content for a scorecard, a yardage book, and the website. It is quoted by the number of holes covered.

Will this work on our existing website?

In almost every case, yes. The scene and the tour embed on the page you already have. If your platform blocks embeds, we tell you before you book rather than after.

Are you insured and certified?

FAA Part 107 certified remote pilot, and we file the airspace authorization your location requires. An uncertificated operator flying your property commercially is not legal, and that exposure lands on the property as well as the operator.

How far do you travel?

Las Vegas and the Brazos Valley are included. We have shot in St. George, Utah, and travel beyond those markets is quoted per project.
